# Describing Cloud Computing

*Companies and businesses that want to offer their services or products to users via the internet tend to build their own IT infrastructure. They must purchase servers, switches, routers, cables, and other devices and also build or rent a space to locate these devices. Then, they pay the cooling and electricity bills.*

*Most importantly, they must hire new employees such as network engineers, administrators, and data center engineers and invest in their knowledge. Other consideration when building your own data center include mandatory software and hardware upgrades, security, data center maintenance, backups, and the cost that accompanies owning your own IT infrastructure.*

*For this reason, more businesses are using cloud computing. Compared to traditional IT infrastructure, the cloud computing approach can be resilient, flexible, secure, and automated without the deployment and maintenance overhead. With cloud computing, servers, networking devices, and other equipment are hosted in the cloud and not on the premises of the businesses using them.*

*The company that offers the cloud services buys, owns, and maintains the equipment and rents it to other companies who use those services. Therefore, this approach can be more cost-effective in certain scenarios. There is no initial investment (or the initial investment is minimal), and the services are paid for on a pay-per-use basis.*

*Companies that provide cloud services normally host their servers in many cities, countries, and continents. The cloud computing approach allows companies to quickly expand globally, an important goal in this always-on digital world.*

## Cloud Computing Overview

*Cloud computing offers companies and individuals convenient models to access infrastructure, platforms, and services on a pay-as-you-go basis. With cloud computing, companies free up capital, streamline IT maintenance, and modernize and scale business approaches. They can build security and flexibility into services and solutions, helping customers in new ways and growing their business in ever-changing market conditions.*

*Cloud computing also allows its users to expand globally in minutes. The companies providing cloud computing services usually host their servers and other equipment in many cities, countries, and continents. Latency for the end users is lower, and the users will have a better user experience.*

## Cloud Computing Principles

*When the centralization concept follows a required set of fundamental IT principles, it becomes a cloud computing model of modern IT infrastructures and data centers.*

*The cloud computing infrastructure principles are as follows:*

- ***Automation:*** *Automation creates time savings and enables user-based self-provisioning of IT services.*

- ***Resilience:*** *Applications running in the cloud, and data, are distributed across many servers. Therefore, no data is lost, and no downtime occurs if a server failure occurs. Extra resilience is also provided by many isolated data centers (availability zones) in a region.*

- ***Cost savings:*** *Since the cloud approach follows pay-as-you-go pricing, and because there is no necessary initial investment, it can be a better option for many businesses.*

- ***Security:*** *Cloud providers have an extensive set of security policies and monitoring practices that makes them more secure than many traditional data centers.*

- ***Flexibility:*** *Clouds provide unlimited resources compared to a traditional data center. Therefore, it is easy to scale your cloud instances and services up and down to increase their performance.*

***The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)*** *defines the cloud as a model for enabling ubiquitous, convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources. Users can rapidly provision and release networks, servers, storage, applications, and services and with minimal management effort or service provider interaction.*

*As you can see in the figure, a centralized cloud can serve many end users with various workloads.*

*Deploying a cloud requires deploying a network of systems that offer computing resources to remote users (business or consumer). Therefore, from a user perspective, the resources are transparently available, regardless of the user point of entry. Examples include a PC at home, an office computer at work, a borrowed computer, or a computer on a school campus. Data that the user stores is available whenever the user connects to the cloud.*

*The resources (storage, computing, or applications) that a cloud offers can vary greatly. In all cases, the resources are delivered as a service rather than a product.*

*Advantages to cloud users:*

- *On-demand, self-service resource provisioning*

- *Application programming interface (API) options*

- *Highly available, horizontally scaled application architectures*

- *Tenant isolation and security*

*Advantages to the cloud provider:*

- *Real cost reduction in operation costs from standardization and automation*

- *Economies of scale through aggregation of customer demand*

- *High utilization through virtualized, shared resources*

- *Customer self-service*

- *Fail-in-place operations model*

*Cloud computing resembles a shared data center that emphasizes virtualization and automation. It results in consolidation gains in hardware capital expenditures (CapEx) and management operational expenditures (OpEx).*

*Benefits to users of cloud services include the appearance of centralized resources that allow a user to move from device to device and experience a familiar environment.*

*Note: Resources can and should be distributed within the cloud infrastructure for better resiliency, which is transparent to the user and creates the appearance of centralized resources.*

*Data that you store in the cloud is always available on-demand. Backups and data management are centralized and managed within the cloud.*

*If cloud computing offers CPU or memory-intensive services as part of the cloud, these resources are cloud-managed. Cloud services may cut equipment costs because user computers may require fewer resources. Also, resources are managed in the cloud rather than at workstations, so application performance is not subject to a user’s workstation configuration.*

## Cloud Computing Characteristics

*NIST identifies cloud computing with the following essential characteristics:*

- ***On-demand self-service:***

	- *A consumer can unilaterally provision computing capabilities.*
	
	- *Server time and network storage are used as necessary.*
	
	- *No human interaction with the service provider is required.*
	
- ***Broad network access:***
	
	- *Promotes use by heterogeneous thin or thick client platforms, such as mobile phones, tablets, laptops, and workstations.*
	
	- *Capabilities are available over the network and are accessed through standard mechanisms.*
	
- ***Resource pooling:***

	- *Computing resources of the provider are pooled to serve multiple consumers.*
	
	- *Uses a multitenant model with different physical and virtual resources.*
	
	- *Resources are dynamically assigned and reassigned according to consumer demand.*
	
	- *The customer can specify the geographical location of the resources (CPU, memory, storage, networks, and virtual machines) to use (at a high level of abstraction, such as country or state).*
	
- ***Rapid elasticity:***

	- *Capabilities can be rapidly and elastically provisioned and released, sometimes automatically, to scale outward and inward with demand.*
	
	- *Capabilities that are available for provisioning often appear unlimited.*
	
	- *Capabilities can be appropriated in any quantity at any time.*
	
- ***Measured service:***

	- *Cloud systems automatically control and optimize resource use with a metering capability at some level of abstraction that is appropriate to the type of service:*
	
	1. *Storage, processing, and bandwidth*
	
	2. *Active user accounts*
	
	- *Both the provider and consumer can transparently monitor, control, and report resource usage.*
	
*A cloud infrastructure can bridge the gap between common single-solution applications of communication technologies and offer an agile, centralized solution.*

![Cloud Computing Characteristics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/cloud_computing_characteristics.png)

*Shared resources in a cloud can be automatically or manually distributed among users according to metering.*

*Metering usually happens on a per-use basis for resource types such as CPU, memory, storage, or bandwidth. This approach allows consumers to grow with little or no overhead or infrastructure investment, while allowing the provider to offer competitive pricing.*

## Cloud Deployment Models

*Providers can deploy cloud services in various ways, depending on the organizational structure. NIST distinguishes four deployment models: private, public, hybrid, and community. These types of implementations mainly focus on the ownership and control of the data that are stored on the underlying cloud hardware. Severe security implications arise when using different deployments, along with differing CapEx and OpEx. Different types of clouds, their advantages and disadvantages, and various service models are discussed here.*

*Cloud deployment models are based on the ownership model of the cloud infrastructure:*

- ***Private cloud:*** *The cloud infrastructure is completely dedicated to a particular organization; there is no public access. Usually, it is located on the premises of the organization in question, although it may be outsourced.*

- ***Public cloud:*** *A third-party provider offers the cloud infrastructure, which is accessible by the general public. The service may charge a fee.*

- ***Hybrid cloud:*** *The cloud infrastructure is a mix of at least two (usually private and public) cloud models. The public cloud often supplements a private cloud.*

- ***Community cloud:*** *Several organizations build the cloud infrastructure for the purpose of addressing shared concerns. The most common example is to provide facilities for mutual disaster recovery sites.*

![Cloud Deployment Models](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/cloud_deployment_models.png)

*From the perspective of intended users, clouds fall in two broad categories: public and private.*

*Like private networks, private clouds are intended only for a single group of users. These users may be one corporation or a private group. The entities that use these clouds may still outsource and not manage them. The main distinction is the lack of public access.*

*Public clouds, in contrast to private clouds, are designed for public use. Public clouds can be free for everyone to use, or there may be a use fee that attaches to the level of service.*

*A combination of two or more public and private clouds may comprise a hybrid cloud. A hybrid cloud may be a solution to certain shortcomings of both private and public clouds. By combining these approaches, it is possible to maximize the advantages of both while minimizing the disadvantages of either.*

*It is also possible to simultaneously use two or more public clouds from various cloud providers, which is known as* ***multicloud.*** *Multicloud has benefits such as potential cost savings, reduced vendor lock-in, and redundancy.*

*Deploying an application into clouds from multiple providers offers extra redundancy because the possibility of outage problems on more than one public cloud simultaneously is almost zero. Customers can also achieve cost savings because some public clouds can provide cheaper instances or better resources-to-cost ratio than others. And the last benefit of multicloud is that you can always choose the cloud where you want to move your application and avoid vendor lock-in.*

***Multicloud also has some drawbacks;*** *the most notable one is complexity. Designing, deploying, and maintaining a multicloud environment is not easy because each cloud vendor provides different services and functionalities. Data is also a problem when considering multicloud. Because data must be stored in multiple clouds, that data must move from one cloud to another. This process normally takes time and can be expensive. In general, you can say that multicloud adoption requires a substantial business reason.*

***A community cloud*** *can be considered halfway between a public and private cloud. It is a multitenant infrastructure that is shared among several organizations from a specific group with common computing concerns but is not open for public use.*

## Characteristics of a Private Cloud Deployment

*A private cloud is an enterprise IT infrastructure that is managed with cloud computing qualities such as self-service, pay-as-you-go charge-back, on-demand provisioning, and the appearance of infinite scalability.*

*Private clouds have the following characteristics:*

- *Consolidation, virtualization, and automation of existing data center resources.*

- *Provisioning and cost-metering interfaces that enable self-service IT consumption.*

- *Application toward one or two noncritical application systems.*

## Private Cloud Advantages

*When a company has decided that a private cloud service is appropriate, the private cloud will scale by pooling IT resources under a single cloud operating system or management platform. This arrangement will enable new architectures to target large-scale activities.*

*You can deploy a private cloud in various ways. It can be solely managed and used by a single organization. It can be deployed and managed in the enterprise data center by an external organization, but used only by this enterprise. It can also be offered as a service by a cloud provider.*

*The main advantage of a private cloud is control over corporate data, security, and performance.*

*A private cloud has several management advantages:*

- ***Control of all resources:*** *This complete control allows for unlimited management of resources. The resources are not shared with other cloud users.*

- ***Control of changes:*** *Full control also permits changes in the cloud immediately; staff knowledge and money are the only constraints. They may apply patches and updates when necessary and not when a cloud operator policy determines the schedule.*

- ***Control of information:*** *Control of information prevents information theft—the most important public cloud issue. If a company controls its own cloud from the hardware and premises, it can implement enough security to ensure that information stays internal. When using public cloud providers, there are few ways that you can guarantee information safety. To a certain extent, you are limited by the ethical and legal restrictions that result from a contract. In a world where corporate espionage is becoming more common, data security can be a significant factor when contemplating a private cloud solution.*

- ***Encryption:*** *It is possible to protect data on a public cloud, or even a private cloud that is outsourced, by encrypting it. This method must rely on end-to-end encryption, where data is encrypted the moment that it leaves the client computer. It is only possible to implement such a solution if the data is not processed inside the cloud. The sole case is the software as a service (SaaS) model, where a terminal device (web browser or thin client) processes data locally. The data is stored only in the cloud. When the cloud infrastructure processes the data, such protection is impossible. Examples include infrastructure as a service (IaaS), platform as a service (PaaS), and some SaaS implementations. The only solution is to implement a private cloud on a private infrastructure.*

## Private Cloud Disadvantages

*A private cloud has some disadvantages:*

- ***Higher cost:*** *Building your own private cloud can be expensive because you must extend CapEx to buy servers, switches, cables, and other devices. You also must pay monthly for electricity, cooling, licenses, and the IT staff who manage your private cloud.*

- ***Maintenance:*** *Maintaining your private cloud solution also takes time and money because of manual software and firmware upgrades and continuous support that normally requires additional in-house IT personnel.*

- ***Underutilization:*** *Because it is hard to predict the utilization levels of your workload when designing your private cloud infrastructure, there is a possibility that your resources may become underutilized.*

- ***Scaling:*** *Scaling is possible with your private cloud, but it takes time and can be complicated. For example, adding a new server to provide more resources can take months because the server must be ordered, shipped, installed, and connected.*

## Characteristics of a Public Cloud Deployment

*A public cloud is provisioned for open use by the general public and managed by a dedicated cloud service provider. A business, academic, or government organization (or some combination of these entities) may own, manage, and operate the public cloud. The public cloud exists on the premises of the cloud provider.*

*A public cloud can bundle IT resources and services with cloud computing qualities, such as self-service, pay-as-you-go billing, on-demand provisioning, and the appearance of infinite scalability.*

*Some examples of public cloud offerings are Amazon Web Services (AWS) with Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (EC2); Google services, including applications such as Google Docs and Gmail; Skype voice services; and Salesforce.com cloud-based customer relationship management (CRM).*

*The figure represents a public cloud where various end users share the same cloud provider over the internet.*

![Public Cloud](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/public_cloud_figure.png)

*Advantages of a public cloud include the following:*

- *Easy and inexpensive setup with all costs covered by the provider.*

- *No wasted resources when you pay only for what you use.*

- *Maintainance by an external provider (a major difference between public and private clouds)*

- *On-demand provisioning*

- *Support for multiple customers*

- *Suited for information that is not sensitive*

*Public clouds are a cheap solution for companies that want cloud services but do not want to invest in their own data center. It may also be favorable to outsource a private cloud, but doing so could still incur much higher costs than a public cloud service.*

*When going with a public cloud service, the user has no CapEx. It is usually only a monthly fee for using the public cloud services.*

*This service is especially suitable for microsized companies that often lack an internal IT department. They may use Internet Service Provider (ISP) email services and offline applications, with their primary storage being a small network-attached storage (NAS) system on their LAN. Storing important data in a cloud can be much easier and safer.*

*For companies deploying a new data center, using a public cloud provider is much faster than purchasing and installing their own equipment.*

*Disadvantages of a public cloud include the following:*

- *There is little control over the stored data and provider data-retention policies.*

- *Depending on a public cloud provider means that you have little to no control over upgrades, security fixes, updates, feature additions, and other aspects.*

- *Trusting your cloud provider with your strategic data may be risky. Relying solely on legal protection may be inadequate, because the the provider’s system lacks transparency under normal circumstances.*

- *Sometimes, legal restrictions prevent you from using public clouds to store client data. For example, storing medical records on a public cloud infrastructure may be prohibited, but an overview is problematic.*

- *You have no control over how the cloud provider implements technologies. The most secure system in the world is useless if improperly configured.*

- *Often cloud providers hire security auditors to review the security of their cloud infrastructure. However, the security reports rarely state any details of the audit process or security implementations, because they are often a guarded secret.*

*Protecting the cloud infrastructure is the cloud provider’s responsibility, and clients cannot take much action to improve security. Two-factor authentication can improve secure login if the cloud provider supports it, and as a customer, you can choose to use it or not use it. However, leaving a cloud provider often means great expense. Therefore, it is important to remember vendor lock-in when choosing a suitable public cloud provider.*

## Characteristics of a Hybrid Cloud Deployment

*Hybrid clouds combine a public and private cloud. They enable you to retain control over data, as with private clouds, but maintain the flexibility and reach of public clouds. For hybrid solutions to provide data protection, you must take great care regarding how you manage traffic. If sensitive data passes public cloud infrastructure, it can still be compromised.*

*Hybrid clouds address resource availability and data protection of the private and public cloud model:*

- ***Resource availability:*** *If you operate a private cloud and are running out of resources, you can offload some tasks to a public cloud infrastructure before upgrading the private cloud. In another scenario, your cloud usage is fairly low, with occasional spikes in resource requirements. Rather than upgrading your cloud hardware and experiencing low utilization, you can offload the requests to a public cloud during spikes. Often, public cloud providers will charge based on resource usage; therefore, low resource usage can result in low public cloud costs.*

- ***Data protection:*** *One drawback of using clouds that others manage is the lack of data control and security. Storing critical data on foreign equipment may be impossible. To solve this issue and still use a cloud service, it is possible to combine a private cloud solution (for critical data) with a public cloud (for other data). All that data is rarely considered mission-critical, so the bulk of it can be stored in a more exposed public cloud. For example, healthcare organizations should store medical records on a secured private cloud. An appointment schedule, however, is not as critical and could be stored on a public cloud. A hybrid cloud may require using two clients, one for each cloud, or a dedicated client that internally manages the connectivity between the two clouds.*

![Hybrid Cloud Infrastructure](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/hybrid_cloud_infrastructure.png)

*A hybrid cloud has the following properties:*

- *The IT infrastructure has cloud computing qualities.*

- *Some services exist internally and some exist externally.*

- *This approach takes advantage of data security in private clouds and scalability in public clouds.*

![Hybrid Cloud Infrastructure 2](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/hybrid_cloud_infrastructure2.png)

*A hybrid cloud is a cloud infrastructure that has two or more distinct cloud infrastructures, such as private, community, or public cloud infrastructures. The cloud infrastructure components remain as unique entities. They are bound together using standardized or proprietary technology that enables data and application portability, such as cloud-bursting for load balancing between clouds.*

*After private and public clouds are accepted, the tendency to connect the clouds forms the hybrid cloud.*

*A hybrid cloud links disparate cloud computing infrastructures such as an enterprise private cloud with a service provider public cloud. A hybrid cloud links disparate cloud computing infrastructures with each other by connecting the individual management infrastructures and allowing the exchange of resources.*

*The hybrid cloud can enable the following options:*

- *Disparate (distinct) cloud environments can use other cloud-system resources.*

- *Federation can occur across the data center and organization boundaries with cloud internetworking.*

*Hybrid clouds provide the following:*

- ***Flexibility:*** *The option to use resources from the private and public clouds.*

- ***Control over spending:*** *The cloud offers cheaper resources.*

- ***Deployment:*** *A combination of strong sides of the private and public clouds.*

*Some organizations are satisfied with putting some IT functions in a public cloud but prefer to keep higher-risk or more-tailored functions in a private cloud or even in-house. This concept is a hybrid cloud solution. You do not have to go to the extreme; for example, some organizations use cloud services for archiving old data but keep operational data close.*

*An example of hybrid cloud usage is a financial company that operates with very sensitive data and experiences spikes when the system must process payroll and other financial data. They store data on their premises where they have full control over it and process the data using the resources of their private cloud on normal days. But at the beginning of each month, when they process payroll data, they use public cloud instances because the resources of their private cloud are inadequate.*

*Security plays an important role in this case. For this reason, they also use data encryption and secure ways to connect their private cloud environment with the public cloud.*

## Characteristics of a Community Cloud Deployment

*The goal of a community cloud for participating organizations is to take advantage of a public cloud with the benefits (privacy, security, and compliance) of a private cloud. Several organizations share the community cloud. These organizations are located either locally or remotely, and the participating organizations or a third-party service provider manages the cloud.*

*One example of a community cloud deployment is healthcare or financial services, which require special regulation.*

*Another community cloud example is the Open Cloud Consortium Biomedical Commons Cloud (BCC). Hospitals and research centers access data in a shared, external data center. This type of access helps medical institutions avoid replicating data center investments, while simultaneously ensuring compliance with strict regulations governing healthcare.*

## Cloud System Architecture

*The system architecture of a cloud is like the architecture of a data center. The cloud has all the components and aspects that a typical data center has.*

*Virtualization delivers tremendous flexibility to build and design data center solutions. The separation of a single user group or a separation of data center resources from the rest of the network should consider the diverse networking needs of different enterprises. Separation tasks become complex when it is impossible to confine specific users or resources to specific areas in the network. When separation occurs, the physical positioning will no longer address the problem.*

![Cloud System Architecture](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/cloud_system_arhitecture.png)

*The virtualized hardware components are located on the lower level, such as the following:*

- ***Storage virtualization:*** *Storage virtualization allows you to pool storage on diverse and independent devices into a single view. Features such as copy services, data migration, and multiprotocol and multivendor integration can benefit from storage virtualization.*

- ***Computing virtualization:*** *This paradigm is used in server deployments. The servers become stateless, with service profiles defining operational properties of the servers, such as MAC addresses, universally unique identifiers (UUIDs), World Wide Names (WWNs), and others. By applying the service profile to another stateless hardware instance, you can move the workload to another server for added capacity or recovery.*

- ***Network virtualization:*** *Network virtualization can address the problem of separation. Network virtualization also provides other types of benefits such as increased network availability, better security, consolidation of multiple networks, segmentation of networks, and increased network availability. Examples of network virtualization are virtual LANs (VLANs) and virtual SANs (VSANs) in Fibre Channel SANs. A VLAN virtualizes the Layer 2 segments, making them independent of the physical topology. This virtualization allows you to connect two servers to the same physical switch, although the servers participate in different logical broadcast domains (VLANs). A similar concept represents a VSAN in Fibre Channel SANs.*

- ***Cloud operating system:*** *The key components of the cloud operating system are the VSAN, VLAN, and voice port module (VPM) along with virtual machines.*

*The virtualized hardware is on the domain abstraction layer of the framework. Storage devices can be virtualized into storage pools, and network devices are virtualized by using device contexts.*

## Infrastructure as a Service

*IaaS or cloud infrastructure services (sometimes also referred to as hardware as a service) can deliver computer infrastructure (typically a platform virtualization environment) as a service. Rather than purchasing servers, software, data center space, or network equipment, clients can buy those resources as a fully outsourced service.*

*An example of using IaaS is using virtual server instances that are hosted on the premises of a cloud computing provider. You choose this option if you have no infrastructure that is suitable for deploying and running virtual machines on your premises. Setting up such an instance begins by selecting a location where you want to deploy your instance and selecting an operating system. Next, you assign CPU, memory, and storage resources to it. Since this process is automated, you are not required to manually configure any IPs and passwords or wait for an operating system to install. It takes a relatively small amount of time to deploy your virtual server.*

*The IaaS model has the following characteristics:*

- *It can provision computing resources.*

- *The consumer can deploy and run arbitrary software.*

- *The consumer does not manage or control the underlying cloud infrastructure.*

- *The services are billed as a utility with a predictable cost structure.*

*Examples of IaaS model services:*

- *AWS Elastic Compute Cloud (Amazon EC2)*

- *Microsoft Azure*

- *Rackspace*

- *IBM Cloud*

- *Google Cloud Infrastructure*

- *Digital Ocean*

- *Linode*

*The figure shows the management distribution of an IaaS service model.*

![IaaS Management Distribution](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/IaaS_management_distribution.png)

*The type of service category also defines the demarcation point for management responsibilities. The management demarcation point ranges from the IaaS model, with shared responsibility between the customer and the service provider, to the SaaS model. In the SaaS model, almost all management responsibilities belong to the service provider.*

*The* ***demarcation points*** *also mean that the service provider is invested with more trust, depending on the model, and must have a better understanding in cases of increased management control. The dependability requirements of the service provider are also higher, depending on the model, and are* ***highest in the SaaS model.***

*You can also see that with* ***IaaS*** *you, as a customer, have no control over the physical server hosting your virtual server instance. For this reason, some cloud hosting providers offer a dedicated server to their customers. The customer has no shared resource security concerns, always experiences consistent performance, and has control over the entire server, including network configuration, storage, and the operating system running on the server.*

*The* ***infrastructure characteristics of IaaS*** *include the following:*

- *A utility computing service and billing model*

- *Automation of administrative tasks*

- *Dynamic scaling*

- *Desktop virtualization*

- *Policy-based services*

- *Internet connectivity*

*The IaaS service is typically billed on a utility computing basis. The amount of resources that are consumed, and therefore the cost, typically reflect the level of activity. IaaS is an evolution of virtual private server offerings.*

## Platform as a Service

*PaaS is the delivery of a computing platform and solution stack as a service. PaaS facilitates the deployment of applications without the cost and complexity of buying and managing the underlying hardware and software and provisioning hosting capabilities. PaaS provides all the facilities that are necessary to support the complete lifecycle of building and delivering web applications and services entirely from the internet.*

*For example, you are a code developer in a small company. Because the code that you are working on is constantly changing, you must implement many changes in the environment where it runs. As a code developer, you do not want to spend time reconfiguring and maintaining infrastructure. In this case, you can use PaaS as a solution. With this model, you set up your environment by selecting a predefined environment configuration from a drop-down list and choosing the amount of CPU, memory, and storage resources for your application. This approach takes less time and effort to bring up your environment, requires no installation of systems, and most importantly allows you to dedicate your time solely to developing your code.*

*The PaaS model has the following characteristics:*

- *It can deploy applications that are created using programming languages, libraries, services, and tools that the provider supports.*

- *The customer does not manage or control the underlying cloud infrastructure.*

*Examples of PaaS services:*

- *AWS Elastic Beanstalk*

- *Red Hat OpenShift*

- *Microsoft Azure*

- *Google App Engine*

- *Heroku*

*Cloud providers offer a computing platform that typically includes an operating system, a programming language execution environment, a database, and a web server.*

*The applications may include facilities for application design, application development, testing, deployment, and hosting. Offerings may also include application services such as team collaboration, web service integration and marshaling, and database integration. Other offers include security, scalability, storage, persistence, state management, application versioning, application instrumentation, and developer community facilitation. These services may be provisioned online as an integrated solution.*

*In a PaaS environment, the following can be offered to a customer:*

- *Application design*

- *Application development*

- *Testing*

- *Deployment*

- *Hosting*

- *Collaboration*

## Software as a Service

*With the SaaS model, cloud service providers offer ready-to-use applications or software to the end customers. The NIST definition of the SaaS service model states that the* ***model allows the consumer to use the provider’s applications, which run on a cloud infrastructure.*** *The applications are accessible from various client devices through a thin-client interface such as a web browser (for example, web-based email) or a program interface.*

*The consumer does not manage or control the underlying cloud infrastructure. This infrastructure includes the network, servers, operating systems, storage, or even individual application capabilities, with the possible exception of limited user-specific application configuration settings.*

*For example, an individual wants to open an online store business to sell homemade products. Because they are not an expert in creating and setting online stores, and because they lack enough revenue to pay for such an expert, they choose a SaaS solution. Their online store is set up in minutes with minimal effort, and they have no concerns about maintenance and upgrades or hiring experts. Most importantly, they pay only a fixed amount of money monthly.*

*The SaaS model has the following characteristics:*

- *Consumers can use the provider applications running on a cloud infrastructure*

- *Applications are accessible from various client devices.*

- *The consumer does not manage or control the underlying cloud infrastructure.*

- *Applications may run partially or completely in the provider environment.*

*Examples of SaaS services:*

- *Cisco Webex*

- *Cisco Meraki*

- *Microsoft 365*

- *Shopify*

- *Salesforce*

- *Slack*

- *Gmail*

*The figure shows the management distribution of a SaaS service model.*

![SaaS Management Distribution](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PurityControl7/DCFNDU/refs/heads/root/images/SaaS_management_distribution.png)

*The SaaS provider may run all or part of the application on the hardware. The SaaS provider may also download executable code to client machines as needed, disabling the code when the customer contract expires. The software can be licensed for a single user or for a group of users.*

*SaaS providers must meter the resources that they offer and customers use, including broadband network traffic, public IP addresses, and storage space. Multiple cost models provide flexibility in measuring costs.*

*The infrastructure characteristics of SaaS include the following:*

- *Cloud providers should operate application software.*

- *Cloud users do not manage the infrastructure and platform.*

- *There is no need to install and run applications on user computers.*

*The management* ***demarcation point*** *in the SaaS model means that the service provider is invested with more trust. The dependability requirements of the service provider are* ***highest in the SaaS model.*** *The cloud provider manages everything.*

## Anything as a Service

*Anything as a service* ***(XaaS)*** *describes everything that can be offered as a cloud-based IT service. XaaS includes a wide array of service categories with a long list of products in each category. The cloud services that are offered are still categorized under the three major service models: IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS. However, the need to give a more granular description of the specific services that the different cloud providers offer led to the adoption and wide usage of XaaS.*

*XaaS describes the growing variety of services that can be offered using the cloud model, including the following:*

- ***DaaS:*** *Desktop as a service*

- ***DRaaS:*** *Disaster recovery as a service*

- ***ITaaS:*** *IT as a service*

- ***STaaS:*** *Storage as a service*

- ***NaaS:*** *Network as a service*

- ***MaaS:*** *Monitoring or management as a service*

- ***UCaaS:*** *Unified communications as a service*

*Examples of XaaS services:*

- *Cisco vDesktop*

- *Amazon WorkSpaces*

- *VMware Horizon Desktop as a Service (DaaS)*
